What I've learned between the factory floor and the boardroom

Nature is a shared asset. It needs shared investment.

Smallholder farmers plant and protect the trees and natural resources that supply chains depend on. Buyers should help pay for those ecosystem services, back zero forest degradation and support a living income. No company can do this alone: it also takes government policy and capital-market support.

The farmer plants the tree. The buyer should help pay for it.

Agroforestry only lasts when the people downstream reward it, through premiums, co-investment and long-term contracts.

Regulation is the floor, not the strategy.

CSRD, EUDR, TNFD and SBTi set the minimum. The same data, used well, sharpens procurement, prices risk and protects margin.

From lab bench to factory line

The decision
Could new meat products be scaled to full production without disrupting daily output, and reach lower-income markets profitably?
What I did
Reporting to the R&D Director, I took three products (pepperoni, Polish sausage and bacon) from formulation to factory. At its core this was food science: working through the chemistry of proteins, fats, salt and binders, trialling functional proteins, starches and enzymes with suppliers, and using physical simulations to predict how each recipe would behave at scale. I also set raw-material specifications with pork and beef plants and negotiated line time with the Plant Manager for trials, often from 3 am.
Result
All three products in industrial production, with ISO 22000, CAPA and FMEA controls built in, in a business with revenues of about £1bn.

Launching a factory with MODAPTS and Kanban

The decision
A new factory had to hit the tonnage behind scheduled regional launches. Selected a month after joining, I was asked to lead production.
What I did
Recruited 25 operatives and three mechanics and secured budget for engineering changes, making the calls on equipment selection and design, including redesigned product elevators and new handling systems. Result
The factory launched on time and on budget, and I later recommissioned a high-value line with specialists from Mexico and Colombia.

      Research: tracing chocolate back to the farm

      My PhD, published as "Broken chocolate" in Supply Chain Management, asked a question with big commercial consequences: once cocoa is blended and processed, can a buyer still prove where it came from?

      Why it matters. Fraud, deforestation, child labour and modern slavery hide in the tiers a buyer can't see. A test that works on the finished product gives procurement teams a way to check claims instead of trusting paperwork.

      Ideas, speaking and press

      Five rules for carbon insetting

      1. Bring partners, customers and regulators in early, with shared KPIs and budgets.
      2. Start with the customers ready to co-invest.
      3. Use secondary data to find where to act; don't wait for perfect primary data.
      4. Choose modular, interoperable tools over one platform for everything.
      5. Frame reductions as supply-chain projects to make co-investment simpler.
